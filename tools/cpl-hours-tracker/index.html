How the tracker counts it (cross-reference, not part of the eGCA table): The Note to Aircraft Rules 1937, Schedule II, Section M, paragraph 1(e) says that on an aeroplane that must fly with a co-pilot, only 50% of ordinary co-pilot time counts towards total flight time. PIC under supervision (PICUS) counts in full towards the total but only 50% towards PIC time. The 50% co-pilot credit on night time and on the last-six-months total is DGCA application-form practice; that Note does not state it. It counts at most 25 h of simulator time towards instrument time, because the form says so and the 50 h in actual flight leaves only 25 h of the 75 h for a simulator.

The government copy of Section M we could open (civilaviation.gov.in) still shows the figures from before 2020: 500 h PIC, 1,000 h cross-country, 100 h instrument and 150 h in the last 12 months. The eGCA figures match the 2020 amendment (G.S.R. 22(E)), so the tracker uses eGCA.

How the hours for a CPL and an ATPL add up

To get a Commercial Pilot Licence (aeroplanes) in India you need at least 200 hours of flight time. The official compilation still counts those hours inside the five years before you apply; a 2019 amendment may have removed that limit, but the final gazette could not be opened when checking, so the tracker keeps the five-year window on unless you turn it off. The other minimums are not extra hours on top. They are slices of the same 200: at least 100 hours as pilot-in-command, 20 of those across country, 10 hours of instrument time and 5 hours at night.

That overlap is why students often have plenty of total time but are still short on one slice. A solo night cross-country counts towards four bars at once; a dual circuit session only adds to the total.

Some rules depend on dates rather than totals. You need 15 hours as PIC and 10 night take-offs and landings as PIC in the six months before you apply. Set the date at the top to your planned application day. Only 5 of the 10 instrument hours may come from an approved simulator, so extra simulator time does not help.

The ATPL works the same way on a bigger scale: 1,500 hours, including 250 as PIC or PICUS, 500 across country, 100 at night, 75 on instruments (50 in actual flight) and, in the six months before you apply, both 10 hours of multi-engine PIC and 20 hours of multi-engine co-pilot time (items 7 and 8 are both required). Not every airline hour counts in full. Ordinary co-pilot time counts at half towards the total, and PICUS counts in full towards the total but at half towards PIC, as in the Note to Section M. The 50% co-pilot credit on night and last-six-month time is DGCA application-form practice, not stated in that Note. Switch to ATPL and the same log is re-counted with these credits.

The finish date is a rough guide. It takes how much of each kind of flying you did in the last 60 days and projects it forward.
