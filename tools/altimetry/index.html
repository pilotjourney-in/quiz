QNH, QFE, QNE and why cold air matters

An altimeter is a barometer that turns pressure into height using the ISA. The number set in the subscale picks the zero point. With QNH set it shows altitude above mean sea level, so it reads the aerodrome elevation on the runway. With QFE set it shows height above the aerodrome and reads zero on the runway. With QNE (1013.25 hPa) it shows pressure altitude, which is what flight levels use above the transition altitude.

Near sea level, 1 hPa is about 27 ft, which gives the quick exam rule: QFE ≈ QNH − elevation ÷ 27. The calculator also uses the full ISA formula, which matters at high aerodromes.

The altimeter assumes ISA temperatures. In air colder than ISA, the pressure levels sit closer together, so you are lower than the altimeter shows. ICAO PANS-OPS has pilots add a correction to minimum altitudes when it is cold. The correction grows with height above the aerodrome and with how far below ISA it is.

QFE = QNH × (1 − 6.8756×10⁻⁶ × elevation_ft)^5.2559 (≈ QNH − elevation/27) Cold correction = H × (15 − t0) ÷ (273 + t0 − 0.5 × 0.0019812 × (H + Hss)) H = height above aerodrome, Hss = aerodrome elevation, t0 = aerodrome temp + 0.0019812 × elevation

Worked example

At a sea-level aerodrome at −10 °C (ISA −25 °C), a procedure altitude 1,000 ft above the aerodrome needs a correction of 1,000 × 25 ÷ (263 − 1) ≈ 95 ft. Rounded up to the next 10 ft, that is +100 ft, which matches the ICAO table. At Delhi (elevation 777 ft) with QNH 1008, QFE ≈ 1008 − 777 ÷ 27 ≈ 979 hPa.

Sources: ICAO Doc 8168 PANS-OPS (altimeter corrections, cold temperature correction formula and table); ICAO Doc 7488 Manual of the ICAO Standard Atmosphere; EUROCONTROL Guidelines for Cold Temperature Corrections by ATS (2014) for the accurate method; FAA Aviation Weather Handbook (FAA-H-8083-28) on altimetry.
