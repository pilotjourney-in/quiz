UTC, IST and sun times for pilots

Aviation runs on UTC, also called Zulu time. METARs, TAFs, NOTAMs and flight plans all use it, so an Indian pilot converts constantly. IST is UTC + 5 hours 30 minutes all year, because India has no daylight saving. Add 5:30 to go from UTC to IST and subtract 5:30 to go back, and watch the date: 2000Z on 1 January is 01:30 IST on 2 January.

Sunrise and sunset matter for VFR flying, night rating hours and when runway lights must be on. They change with latitude and the time of year. In December, Leh sees the sun for more than an hour less than Thiruvananthapuram. They also change with longitude, because the whole country shares one clock: the sun rises in Guwahati about an hour and a quarter earlier than in Ahmedabad.

This calculator uses the NOAA solar algorithm. It works out the sun's declination and the equation of time for the date, then the hour angle at which the sun's centre is 0.833° below the horizon. That 0.833° allows for atmospheric refraction and the sun's radius. Civil twilight is when the centre is 6° below.

IST = UTC + 05:30 Solar noon (UTC, min) = 720 − 4 × longitude(°E) − equation of time cos HA = cos 90.833° ÷ (cos φ · cos δ) − tan φ · tan δ Sunrise = solar noon − 4 × HA, Sunset = solar noon + 4 × HA

Worked example

At Delhi (VIDP, 28.56°N 77.10°E) on 21 December 2026, the declination is about −23.4° and the equation of time is about +2 min. Solar noon is 720 − 308.4 − 2 ≈ 409.6 min UTC, which is 06:50Z or 12:20 IST. HA is about 77.4°, so sunrise is about 310 min earlier, at roughly 01:40Z (07:10 IST), and sunset is at about 17:29 IST.

Sources: NOAA Global Monitoring Laboratory Solar Calculator and "General Solar Position Calculations"; ICAO Annex 1 definition of night; airport coordinates from OurAirports open data.
