How it is calculated

Formulae used

  • Pressure altitude = elevation + (1013.25 − QNH) × (1000 ÷ 33.86389) ft.
  • ISA temperature = 15 − 2 × (pressure altitude ÷ 1000) °C.
  • ISA deviation = OAT − ISA.
  • Density altitude ≈ pressure altitude + 120 × (OAT − ISA) ft.
  • The VIDP example uses the published elevation of 777 ft.

How density altitude is estimated

Density altitude is the pressure altitude corrected for temperature. It is the altitude in the International Standard Atmosphere at which the air would have the same density as the air around the aircraft. Pilots use it because a hot, high or low-pressure airfield behaves like a higher one: the take-off roll grows, climb rate falls and true airspeed for a given indicated airspeed is higher. The method on this page is the rule of thumb taught in many DGCA and FAA study courses, not a certified performance calculation.

Pressure altitude is field elevation plus a correction for QNH. This calculator uses 1000 feet for every 33.86389 hectopascals, which is the usual 1000 feet per inch of mercury method with the ICAO inch-to-hectopascal factor. Standard sea-level pressure is 1013.25 hectopascals, so when QNH is below standard the pressure altitude is above the elevation. ISA temperature is 15 degrees Celsius at sea level, falling two degrees for every thousand feet of pressure altitude. Density altitude is then pressure altitude plus 120 feet for every degree the outside air is warmer than ISA, or minus 120 feet for every degree it is colder.

The worked example uses the published elevation of Delhi’s Indira Gandhi International Airport, 777 feet, with a slightly below-standard QNH of 1008 hectopascals and a hot afternoon temperature of 38 degrees Celsius. Those inputs are typical of a pre-monsoon day at VIDP and give a density altitude well above the field elevation. Always check the aircraft flight manual for real performance figures.
