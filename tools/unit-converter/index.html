How it is calculated

Constants used

  • 1 international nautical mile = 1852 metres exactly, so 1 knot = 1.852 km/h.
  • 1 foot = 0.3048 metres exactly.
  • 1 statute mile = 1609.344 metres exactly.
  • 1 inHg = 33.86389 hPa (ICAO conventional value).
  • °F = °C × 9/5 + 32.
  • 1 kg = 2.2046226218 lb.
  • Jet A-1 mass = volume × density. The default density is 0.8 kg/L and can be edited.

How aviation units convert

Pilots move between several unit systems in the same sitting. A METAR gives wind in knots and visibility in metres or kilometres, an altimeter setting may be in hectopascals or inches of mercury, and a fuel slip may be in litres, kilograms or pounds. This converter keeps the official SI and ICAO factors in one place so you can check exam arithmetic and classroom exercises without flipping between tables.

Speed uses the international nautical mile of exactly 1852 metres, so one knot is exactly 1.852 kilometres per hour. Statute miles use the international mile of 1609.344 metres. Altitude and short distances use the international foot of exactly 0.3048 metres. Pressure uses the conventional ICAO factor of 33.86389 hectopascals in one inch of mercury; standard sea-level pressure is 1013.25 hectopascals, which is close to 29.92 inches. Temperature follows the usual formula: Fahrenheit equals Celsius times nine fifths, plus 32. Mass uses the avoirdupois pound, 2.2046226218 pounds in one kilogram.

The Jet A-1 section turns volume into mass, or the other way around, at a density you can edit. The default 0.8 kilograms per litre is a common planning figure, not a figure from a particular batch. Real Jet A-1 density changes with temperature, so replace the default when you have a value from a fuel slip or the aircraft manual. US gallons convert at 3.785411784 litres exactly. The Try an example button fills a 120 knot true airspeed, a typical study figure for a light aeroplane cruise, and shows the kilometre-per-hour equivalent in the result card.
