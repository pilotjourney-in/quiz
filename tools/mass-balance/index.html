How a CG calculation works

Every item on board, from the empty aircraft to the fuel, has a mass and an arm: its distance from a reference line called the datum. Multiply the two and you get its moment, the turning effect it has about the datum. Add up all the masses and all the moments, then divide total moment by total mass. The result is the centre of gravity (CG), the single point where the whole aircraft balances.

The CG must stay between the forward and aft limits in the flight manual. Too far forward and you may not be able to raise the nose; too far aft and the aircraft becomes unstable and hard to recover from a stall. Fuel burns off in flight, so check the landing CG as well as the take-off CG.

Fuel is usually measured by volume, so convert it to mass with specific gravity (SG): litres × SG gives kilograms. AVGAS 100LL is about 0.72 and Jet A-1 about 0.80. Larger aircraft express CG as a percentage of the mean aerodynamic chord (%MAC).

Moment = mass × arm CG = Σ moments ÷ Σ masses Fuel mass (kg) = volume (L) × SG (1 US gal = 3.785 L, 1 Imp gal = 4.546 L) %MAC = (CG − LEMAC) ÷ MAC × 100

Worked example

Basic empty mass 760 kg at 1.00 m, two people 150 kg at 0.94 m, 10 kg of baggage at 2.41 m and 150 L of AVGAS (150 × 0.72 = 108 kg) at 1.22 m. Moments: 760.0 + 141.0 + 24.1 + 131.76 = 1,056.86 kg·m. Total mass 1,028 kg. CG = 1,056.86 ÷ 1,028 = 1.028 m aft of the datum.

Sources: FAA Aircraft Weight and Balance Handbook (FAA-H-8083-1B); FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Chapter 10 Weight and Balance; standard ATPL/DGCA Mass & Balance texts for %MAC.
