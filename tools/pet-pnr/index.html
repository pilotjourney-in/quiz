PET and PNR in plain words

The point of equal time (PET), also called the critical point, is the place on the route where flying on to the destination takes exactly as long as turning back. It matters if something goes wrong, such as a medical emergency or an engine failure, because past the PET it is quicker to continue. With a headwind on the way out, the PET moves towards the destination, since the trip home is downwind and faster.

The point of no return (PNR) is the furthest you can fly out and still get back to the departure airfield with the fuel you have, not counting reserves. It depends on endurance, whereas the PET does not depend on fuel at all.

O = TAS − headwind (groundspeed out) H = TAS + headwind (groundspeed home) PET distance from departure = D × H ÷ (O + H) PNR time = Endurance × H ÷ (O + H), PNR distance = PNR time × O ETA = departure time + D ÷ O, IST = UTC + 5 h 30 min

For an engine-out PET, use the reduced TAS for both O and H in the PET formula. This calculator does that when you fill the optional PET TAS box.

Worked example

A 500 NM leg, TAS 200 kt, 20 kt headwind outbound and 4 hours of safe endurance. O = 180 kt and H = 220 kt. PET = 500 × 220 ÷ 400 = 275 NM from departure, reached after 1 h 32 min. PNR time = 4 × 220 ÷ 400 = 2.2 h (2 h 12 min), so the PNR is 2.2 × 180 = 396 NM out.

Sources: standard ATPL/DGCA Flight Planning & Monitoring and General Navigation texts (PET/critical point and PNR formulas); IST offset per Indian Standard Time (UTC+05:30).
