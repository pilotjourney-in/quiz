How to read a METAR

A METAR is a routine aerodrome weather report. Busy Indian aerodromes issue one every 30 minutes, and a SPECI (special report) when conditions change significantly in between. The groups always come in the same order, set by ICAO Annex 3 and the WMO code form FM 15:

TYPE STATION DAY+TIME(UTC) WIND VISIBILITY RVR WEATHER CLOUD TEMP/DEW QNH TREND

Wind direction is in degrees true, speed in knots (G = gusts). Visibility is in metres, and 9999 means 10 km or more. Cloud amount is FEW, SCT, BKN or OVC, with the base in hundreds of feet above the aerodrome. M before a temperature means minus. The trend covers the next 2 hours: NOSIG (no significant change), BECMG (becoming) or TEMPO (temporarily). CAVOK replaces visibility, weather and cloud when visibility is 10 km or more, there is no cloud below 5,000 ft or the highest minimum sector altitude, no CB or TCU, and no significant weather.

Worked example

METAR VIDP 260230Z COR 28003KT 1600 R28/P2000 BR NSC 13/09 Q1019 BECMG 1500 BR

A corrected report for Delhi on the 26th at 0230 UTC, which is 08:00 IST (UTC + 5 h 30 min). Wind from 280° at 3 kt. Visibility 1,600 m, RVR on runway 28 more than 2,000 m, mist (BR) and no significant cloud. Temperature 13 °C, dew point 9 °C, QNH 1019 hPa. In the next 2 hours visibility is expected to become 1,500 m in mist.

In a north Indian winter, watch the gap between temperature and dew point. When it closes to 1 or 2 °C, mist (visibility 1,000 to 5,000 m) often thickens into fog (FG, below 1,000 m). In dry air, haze (HZ) and smoke (FU) are the usual reasons for poor visibility.

Sources: ICAO Annex 3, Meteorological Service for International Air Navigation, Appendix 3 (METAR/SPECI); WMO-No. 306 Manual on Codes, Vol. I.1, FM 15 METAR and FM 16 SPECI; AIP India GEN 3.5 (meteorological services, IMD). Sample reports from the aviationweather.gov text data service.
