How TAS, CAS and Mach number are linked

Your airspeed indicator measures the difference between pitot (total) and static pressure, and it is calibrated as if you were always at sea level in the ISA. That reading, corrected for instrument and position error, is calibrated airspeed (CAS). As you climb, the air gets thinner and colder, so the same CAS means you are moving faster through the air. That real speed is true airspeed (TAS).

Mach number is TAS divided by the local speed of sound, and the speed of sound depends only on temperature. This calculator first turns CAS into impact pressure, divides it by the ISA static pressure at your pressure altitude to get Mach, then multiplies Mach by the speed of sound at your actual OAT to get TAS. Because it uses the compressible formula, it stays accurate at jet speeds where the simple density method overstates TAS.

a (kt) = 38.967854 × √(OAT + 273.15) qc/P0 = (1 + 0.2 (CAS/661.48)²)^3.5 − 1 M = √(5 [ (qc/P + 1)^(2/7) − 1 ]), P/P0 = δ = (1 − 6.8756×10⁻⁶ h)^5.2559 TAS = M × a EAS = 661.48 × M × √δ

Worked example

An A320 at FL350 with OAT −45 °C flies at 270 kt CAS. ISA at FL350 is −54.3 °C, so it is ISA+9. δ = 0.2353, giving M = 0.795. The speed of sound at −45 °C is 588.6 kt, so TAS = 0.795 × 588.6 ≈ 468 kt. The exam rule of thumb (add 2% per 1,000 ft) would give 459 kt, which is close but ignores temperature and compressibility.

Sources: ICAO Doc 7488 Manual of the ICAO Standard Atmosphere; FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (FAA-H-8083-25), Chapter 8 Flight Instruments.
