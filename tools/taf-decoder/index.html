How to read a TAF

A TAF is an aerodrome forecast. It starts like a METAR (station and issue time in UTC), then gives a validity period such as 2606/2712: from the 26th at 0600 UTC to the 27th at 1200 UTC. Indian TAFs are usually 9 or 30 hours long. Next come the base conditions (wind, visibility, weather, cloud), sometimes with TX and TN for the forecast maximum and minimum temperature. Change groups follow:

BECMG ddhh/ddhh a lasting change at some time in the period (normally within 2 h, never more than 4 h) TEMPO ddhh/ddhh fluctuations, each under 1 hour, together less than half the period FMddhhmm from this time, everything that follows replaces all earlier conditions PROB30 / PROB40 30% or 40% probability, used alone or with TEMPO (PROB30 TEMPO), never with BECMG or FM

After BECMG or TEMPO, only the elements that change are given. Anything not mentioned carries on from before.

Worked example

TAF VECC 260500Z 2606/2712 34007KT 3200 HZ FEW100 BECMG 2617/2619 00000KT 2400 BR BECMG 2623/2701 35005KT 1200 BR

Kolkata, issued on the 26th at 0500 UTC (10:30 IST), valid 0600 UTC on the 26th to 1200 UTC on the 27th. To start with, wind from 340° at 7 kt, visibility 3,200 m in haze and a few clouds at 10,000 ft. Some time between 1700 and 1900 UTC (22:30 IST and 00:30 IST) the wind becomes calm and the visibility 2,400 m in mist. Between 2300 and 0100 UTC it becomes 350° at 5 kt with 1,200 m in mist. The FEW100 cloud is not repeated, so it is still forecast.

Sources: ICAO Annex 3, Meteorological Service for International Air Navigation, Appendix 5 (TAF) and its Table A5-1; WMO-No. 306 Manual on Codes, Vol. I.1, FM 51 TAF; AIP India GEN 3.5 (meteorological services, IMD). Sample reports from the aviationweather.gov text data service.
