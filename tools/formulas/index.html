Air Navigation 10

#1 in 60 rule: track error

Track error (°) = distance off track × 60 ÷ distance flown
  • distance off track NM
  • distance flown NM, from the last fix

Worked example 4 NM right of track after 40 NM: track error = 4 × 60 ÷ 40 = 6° (aircraft is tracking 6° right of the planned track).

Accurate for angles up to about 20°; based on 1 radian ≈ 57.3° ≈ 60°.

Try it: 1 in 60 calculator

#1 in 60 rule: closing angle and total correction

Closing angle (°) = distance off track × 60 ÷ distance to go Heading change to reach destination = track error + closing angle
  • distance to go NM, to the destination or next waypoint

Worked example Same aircraft, 60 NM still to go: closing angle = 4 × 60 ÷ 60 = 4°. Alter heading 6° + 4° = 10° left to arrive at the destination.

Try it: 1 in 60 calculator

#Crosswind and headwind components

Crosswind = W × sin θ Headwind = W × cos θ
  • W wind speed, kt
  • θ angle between wind direction and runway direction, °

Worked example Runway 28 (280°), wind 310° at 18 kt: θ = 30°. Crosswind = 18 × sin 30° = 9.0 kt; headwind = 18 × cos 30° = 15.6 kt.

Use runway magnetic direction with the magnetic wind given by ATC/ATIS; METAR winds are true.

Try it: Crosswind calculator

#Wind triangle: drift (wind correction angle) and ground speed

WCA = sin⁻¹( W × sin(wind angle) ÷ TAS ) Heading = course ± WCA (into the wind) GS = TAS × cos WCA − W × cos(wind angle)
  • W wind speed, kt
  • wind angle wind direction − course, °
  • TAS true airspeed, kt
  • GS ground speed, kt

Worked example Course 240°T, TAS 120 kt, wind 310°T/20 kt: wind angle 70°. WCA = sin⁻¹(20 × sin 70° ÷ 120) = 9.0°, so true heading ≈ 249°. GS = 120 × cos 9.0° − 20 × cos 70° = 111.7 kt.

This example gives 249° true. The same case on an E6B is often shown as 248° magnetic, because magnetic variation is applied.

Try it: Wind triangle (E6B)

#Speed, distance and time

Distance = GS × time Time = distance ÷ GS
  • GS ground speed, kt (NM per hour)
  • time hours (minutes ÷ 60)

Worked example GS 450 kt for 1 h 20 min: distance = 450 × 80 ÷ 60 = 600 NM.

Try it: PET and PNR calculator

#Departure (east–west distance)

Departure (NM) = change of longitude (minutes) × cos(latitude)
  • change of longitude minutes of arc (1° = 60′)
  • latitude ° (mean latitude if it changes)

Worked example 1° (60′) of longitude along 30°N: departure = 60 × cos 30° = 52.0 NM.

#Convergency and conversion angle

Convergency = change of longitude × sin(mean latitude) Conversion angle = ½ × convergency
  • change of longitude °
  • mean latitude °

Worked example Two meridians 20° apart, mean latitude 30°N: convergency = 20 × sin 30° = 10°; conversion angle = 5°.

Great-circle track minus rhumb-line track equals the conversion angle (for short distances).

#Arc to time (longitude and time)

Time (h) = longitude (°) ÷ 15 (15° = 1 h, 1° = 4 min, 1′ = 4 s)
  • longitude ° east (+) or west (−) of Greenwich

Worked example Delhi, 77°13′E: 77.22 ÷ 15 = 5 h 8.9 min ahead of UTC in local mean time. IST is based on 82.5°E: 82.5 ÷ 15 = 5 h 30 min.

Try it: UTC/IST and sunrise calculator

#Point of equal time (PET)

Distance to PET = D × H ÷ (O + H)
  • D total distance, NM
  • O ground speed on (continuing), kt
  • H ground speed home (returning), kt

Worked example D = 800 NM, O = 400 kt, H = 500 kt: PET = 800 × 500 ÷ 900 = 444.4 NM from departure.

Try it: PET and PNR calculator

#Point of no return (PNR)

Time to PNR = E × H ÷ (O + H) Distance to PNR = time to PNR × O
  • E safe endurance (fuel available minus reserves), h
  • O ground speed out, kt
  • H ground speed home, kt

Worked example E = 4 h, O = 400 kt, H = 500 kt: time = 4 × 500 ÷ 900 = 2.222 h = 2 h 13 min; distance = 2.2222 × 400 = 888.9 NM.

Try it: PET and PNR calculator

Meteorology and ISA 5

#ISA temperature at altitude

ISA temp (°C) = 15 − 1.98 × (altitude in ft ÷ 1,000) (up to 36,090 ft; −56.5 °C above)
  • altitude pressure altitude, ft

Worked example FL350: 15 − 1.98 × 35 = −54.3 °C (exam rounding: −55 °C using 2 °C per 1,000 ft).

ICAO Doc 7488: lapse rate 6.5 °C/km = 1.98 °C per 1,000 ft; tropopause 11 km (36,089 ft).

Try it: TAS, CAS and Mach calculator

#ISA deviation

ISA deviation = actual OAT − ISA temperature at that level
  • OAT outside (static) air temperature, °C

Worked example FL350, OAT −45 °C: deviation = −45 − (−54.3) = +9.3 °C, written ISA+9.

Try it: TAS, CAS and Mach calculator

#Pressure altitude from QNH

Pressure altitude = elevation + (1013.25 − QNH) × 27 ft
  • elevation ft
  • QNH hPa
  • 27 ft height of 1 hPa near sea level (some texts use 30 ft)

Worked example Delhi (VIDP), elevation 777 ft, QNH 1008 hPa: PA = 777 + 5.25 × 27 = 919 ft.

Try it: Density altitude calculator

#Density altitude (rule of thumb)

Density altitude ≈ pressure altitude + 120 × (OAT − ISA temp at that pressure altitude)
  • OAT °C
  • 120 ft per °C of ISA deviation (approximation)

Worked example VIDP, PA 919 ft, OAT 38 °C: ISA there = 13.2 °C, deviation +24.8 °C. DA ≈ 919 + 120 × 24.8 = 3,900 ft.

Try it: Density altitude calculator

#Cumulus cloud base from temperature and dew point

Cloud base (ft above ground) ≈ (T − Td) × 400
  • T surface temperature, °C
  • Td surface dew point, °C

Worked example METAR 30/22: base ≈ (30 − 22) × 400 = 3,200 ft (dry adiabatic 3 °C and dew point 0.5 °C per 1,000 ft close 2.5 °C per 1,000 ft).

Try it: METAR decoder

Instruments and altimetry 5

#TAS from CAS (rule of thumb)

TAS ≈ CAS × (1 + 0.02 × altitude in thousands of ft)
  • CAS calibrated airspeed, kt
  • altitude pressure altitude, ft

Worked example CAS 150 kt at 8,000 ft: TAS ≈ 150 × 1.16 = 174 kt (2% per 1,000 ft; ignores temperature and compressibility).

Try it: TAS, CAS and Mach calculator

#Mach number and local speed of sound

M = TAS ÷ a a (kt) ≈ 38.97 × √T(K)
  • a local speed of sound, kt
  • T(K) static air temperature in kelvin (°C + 273.15)

Worked example FL350, OAT −45 °C = 228.15 K: a = 38.97 × √228.15 = 588.6 kt. TAS 460 kt → M = 460 ÷ 588.6 = 0.782.

a = √(γRT): 661.5 kt at 15 °C. The speed of sound depends only on temperature.

Try it: TAS, CAS and Mach calculator

#QFE from QNH

QFE ≈ QNH − elevation ÷ 27 (hPa, elevation in ft)
  • QFE pressure at aerodrome level, hPa
  • elevation aerodrome elevation, ft

Worked example VIDP, elevation 777 ft, QNH 1008: QFE ≈ 1008 − 777 ÷ 27 = 979.2 hPa.

Try it: QNH, QFE and cold-temperature calculator

#Wrong altimeter setting (“high to low, beware below”)

Altitude error (ft) = (setting on subscale − actual QNH) × 27
  • setting on subscale hPa set by the pilot
  • actual QNH hPa

Worked example 1013 set, actual QNH 1003: the aircraft is 270 ft lower than the altimeter shows.

Try it: QNH, QFE and cold-temperature calculator

#Cold temperature correction (4% rule)

Correction ≈ height above aerodrome × 4% × (ISA deviation ÷ 10 °C) (add to minimum altitudes)
  • height above aerodrome ft
  • ISA deviation °C below ISA at the aerodrome

Worked example Minimum altitude 1,500 ft above the aerodrome, aerodrome ISA −20 °C: correction ≈ 1,500 × 0.04 × 2 = 120 ft.

Rule of thumb from ICAO Doc 8168; the calculator also gives the exact ICAO formula.

Try it: QNH, QFE and cold-temperature calculator

Flight Planning 6

#Trip fuel from fuel flow

Fuel = fuel flow × time
  • fuel flow kg/h
  • time h

Worked example 2,400 kg/h for 1 h 45 min: fuel = 2,400 × 1.75 = 4,200 kg.

#Fuel mass from volume and specific gravity

Mass (kg) = volume (litres) × specific gravity 1 US gallon = 3.785 L; 1 imperial gallon = 4.546 L
  • specific gravity kg per litre (Jet A-1 ≈ 0.80, Avgas ≈ 0.72)

Worked example 5,000 L of Jet A-1 at SG 0.80 = 4,000 kg. 100 US gal = 378.5 L.

Try it: Mass and balance calculator

#Top of descent (3° rule)

Descent distance (NM) ≈ height to lose (ft) ÷ 300
  • height to lose ft
  • 300 ft/NM 3° path ≈ 318 ft per NM, rounded

Worked example FL350 to 2,000 ft: (35,000 − 2,000) ÷ 300 = 110 NM before the point where you want to be at 2,000 ft.

Try it: Descent planner

#Rate of descent on a 3° glide path

ROD (ft/min) ≈ GS (kt) × 5 Exact: ROD = GS × 101.27 × tan(glide angle)
  • GS ground speed, kt
  • 101.27 ft/min per kt (6,076 ft ÷ 60)

Worked example GS 140 kt: ROD ≈ 140 × 5 = 700 ft/min (exact for 3°: 743 ft/min).

Try it: Descent planner

#Rate of descent or climb from a gradient

ROD (ft/min) = GS (kt) ÷ 60 × gradient (ft/NM)
  • gradient ft per NM

Worked example GS 420 kt on a 318 ft/NM path: ROD = 420 ÷ 60 × 318 = 2,226 ft/min.

Try it: Descent planner

#Climb gradient (%)

Gradient (%) = ROC ÷ (GS × 101.27) × 100
  • ROC rate of climb, ft/min
  • GS ground speed, kt

Worked example ROC 700 ft/min at GS 100 kt: 700 ÷ 10,127 × 100 = 6.9%.

Mass and Balance 4

#Moment and centre of gravity

Moment = mass × arm CG = total moment ÷ total mass
  • arm distance from the datum, m (or in)
  • moment kg·m

Worked example Empty 1,100 kg at 2.10 m, crew 170 kg at 2.05 m, fuel 150 kg at 1.85 m: total 1,420 kg, moment 2,936.0 kg·m, CG = 2,936.0 ÷ 1,420 = 2.068 m aft of datum.

Try it: Mass and balance calculator

#CG as % of MAC

%MAC = (CG − LEMAC) ÷ MAC × 100
  • LEMAC leading edge of the mean aerodynamic chord, distance from datum
  • MAC length of the mean aerodynamic chord

Worked example CG 18.0 m, LEMAC 16.5 m, MAC 4.2 m: (18.0 − 16.5) ÷ 4.2 × 100 = 35.7% MAC.

Try it: Mass and balance calculator

#CG shift when a load moves

ΔCG = mass moved × distance moved ÷ total mass
  • mass moved kg
  • distance moved m
  • total mass kg, aircraft total

Worked example 100 kg moved 5 m aft in a 2,000 kg aircraft: ΔCG = 100 × 5 ÷ 2,000 = 0.25 m aft.

Try it: Mass and balance calculator

#Mass to add (or remove) to move the CG

Mass to add = total mass × (new CG − old CG) ÷ (arm of added mass − new CG)
  • arm of added mass m from datum

Worked example 2,000 kg at CG 2.05 m; to reach 2.10 m by loading at 3.0 m: 2,000 × 0.05 ÷ 0.90 = 111.1 kg.

Try it: Mass and balance calculator

Performance and Principles of Flight 5

#Lift (and drag) equation

L = ½ ρ V² S CL (drag: D = ½ ρ V² S CD)
  • ρ air density, kg/m³ (1.225 at ISA sea level)
  • V true airspeed, m/s
  • S wing area, m²
  • CL lift coefficient

Worked example ρ 1.225, V 60 m/s, S 16 m², CL 0.5: L = 0.5 × 1.225 × 3,600 × 16 × 0.5 = 17,640 N (about 1,799 kg).

#Load factor and stall speed in a turn

n = 1 ÷ cos(bank angle) Stall speed in turn = Vs × √n
  • n load factor, g
  • Vs wings-level stall speed, kt

Worked example 60° bank: n = 1 ÷ cos 60° = 2.0 g. Vs 50 kt becomes 50 × √2 = 70.7 kt.

#Radius of turn

r (ft) = V² ÷ (11.26 × tan bank) (V in kt TAS)
  • V true airspeed, kt
  • 11.26 g converted for kt and ft

Worked example 120 kt at 30° bank: r = 14,400 ÷ (11.26 × tan 30°) = 2,215 ft (about 0.36 NM).

#Bank angle for a rate 1 turn

Bank (°) ≈ TAS (kt) ÷ 10 + 7 (rate 1 = 3°/s, 2 min for 360°)
  • TAS true airspeed, kt

Worked example TAS 120 kt: bank ≈ 12 + 7 = 19° (exact tan⁻¹(V·ω/g) = 18.2°).

#Speeds change with mass

V₂ = V₁ × √(M₂ ÷ M₁) (same CL: stall speed, best-glide speed, etc.)
  • V₁, M₁ known speed and mass
  • M₂ new mass

Worked example Vs 120 kt at 60 t; at 54 t: 120 × √(54/60) = 113.8 kt.

How to use this sheet

The DGCA CPL papers mix exact formulas with rules of thumb, such as 1 in 60, 300 ft per NM, ROD = GS × 5 and the 4% cold-temperature rule. Learn both: the exam options are usually far enough apart that the rule of thumb picks the right answer, and the calculators show how close it is to the exact value. Work in the units shown. Most mistakes come from mixing feet and metres, or minutes and hours.

Sources: ICAO Doc 7488 (standard atmosphere), ICAO Doc 8168 PANS-OPS (cold-temperature correction), FAA Pilot's Handbook of Aeronautical Knowledge (aerodynamics, weight and balance), standard ATPL/DGCA navigation texts. Every worked example on this page is recalculated by a test script.
