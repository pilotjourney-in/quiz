Frequently asked questions

What is Pilot Journey? Pilot Journey is a study platform for Indian pilots preparing for DGCA CPL and ATPL theory papers, and Airbus A320 and Boeing 737 systems. Every quiz includes an explanation, so you learn the reason behind the answer.

Is Pilot Journey free to use? Yes. Every subject, quiz, mock exam and explanation is free, and progress is saved in your browser. There is no account and no advertising.

Which subjects are covered? Air Regulations, Meteorology, Air Navigation, Radio Navigation, Instrumentation, Principles of Flight, Performance, Powerplant, A320 Systems, Flight Planning & Monitoring, Mass & Balance, B737 NG Systems, B737 MAX Systems. More subjects are added as each one is checked.

Does Pilot Journey use previous DGCA question papers? No. The bank is written around the published DGCA CPL and ATPL syllabus and standard ATA system knowledge. It is original practice material, not a reproduction of live or leaked examination papers.

How do mock exams work? Each subject has a timed mock exam that draws up to 20 questions from across its chapters. You get 25 minutes and need 70% to pass, matching the per-subject DGCA pass standard. Practice mode, by contrast, reveals the explanation as you go.

How is my progress saved? Attempts, scores and flagged questions are stored locally in this browser. Clearing your browser data will clear your flight log, so use the same browser and device for a continuous record.

What is the daily pilot check? Ten mixed questions, refreshed at midnight, scored on accuracy and time. It is the quickest way to keep your knowledge warm on a day without a full study session.

Can I practise only the questions I got wrong? Yes. Flag any question during a quiz and it appears in your flight log. You can then run a revision session made up only of flagged questions until each one is clear.

Is Pilot Journey affiliated with DGCA, Airbus or the airlines? No. Pilot Journey is an independent study project. DGCA, Airbus, IndiGo, Air India and other names are referenced only to describe the exams and aircraft the material is relevant to.
